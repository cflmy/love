# QDQC Cinematic Direction

## Cinematic Shot Bible · v2.0

> **For directors / AI / engineers / animation designers**
>
> This document does not describe implementation.
> It describes how QDQC should be **filmed**.
>
> The website is not a collection of pages.
>
> It is a continuous film whose camera is controlled by the user's scrolling.

---

# 00 · Director's Principle

QDQC should feel like:

> **一部可以被用户亲手翻动的东方爱情电影。**

The user is not scrolling through content.

The user is:

> **walking through memories.**

Therefore every scroll movement should answer one of four questions:

1. **What does the camera discover?**
2. **What does the world reveal?**
3. **What does the character feel?**
4. **What changes between this moment and the previous moment?**

If scrolling only reveals another image or another paragraph, the shot has failed.

---

# 01 · Global Cinematic Grammar

## 01.1 Scroll = Camera

Never think:

```text
scroll → next section
```

Think:

```text
scroll
→ camera moves
→ world changes
→ subject moves
→ light changes
→ atmosphere changes
→ story advances
```

A section boundary should be invisible whenever possible.

The user should feel:

> “我正在穿过这个世界。”

rather than:

> “我进入了下一页。”

---

# 01.2 Spatial Motion Has Priority

Animation priority:

```text
Camera movement
    ↓
Depth / parallax
    ↓
Subject movement
    ↓
Lighting
    ↓
Atmosphere
    ↓
Typography
    ↓
Opacity
```

Opacity is the weakest cinematic tool.

Use it only when spatial transformation is insufficient.

---

# 01.3 Every Shot Needs a Foreground

A cinematic shot should rarely be:

```text
background + character
```

Prefer:

```text
foreground
     ↓
midground
     ↓
subject
     ↓
background
```

Foreground can be:

* branch
* flower
* mist
* particle
* curtain
* water reflection
* mountain edge
* cloud
* architectural element

Foreground gives the camera something to travel through.

---

# 01.4 Every Shot Needs a Subject

The viewer must know where to look.

Motion hierarchy:

```text
Primary subject
    ↓
Secondary subject
    ↓
Environment
    ↓
Atmosphere
```

Never animate everything equally.

---

# 01.5 Every Shot Has a Breath

A good shot has:

```text
anticipation
→ movement
→ discovery
→ settling
```

Do not keep accelerating continuously.

The camera should sometimes stop.

Silence is part of the animation.

---

# 02 · Opening / NFC

## Emotional purpose

The website begins before the story begins.

The NFC tap is not merely authentication.

It is:

> **打开一封信。**

The user should feel that something dormant has awakened.

---

# Shot 02.1 · Darkness

### Visual

Pure darkness.

Do not immediately show the moon.

There should be a tiny amount of atmospheric noise.

Almost invisible.

### Camera

Static.

Extremely slow forward drift.

The movement should be almost imperceptible.

### Sound

One very distant breath / wind.

Then silence.

### Duration

Approximately 1–2 seconds of perceived time.

The user should have a moment to notice:

> “Something is waiting.”

---

# Shot 02.2 · First Light

A tiny point of warm light appears.

Not a fade-in circle.

It should feel like:

> something has been touched awake.

The light expands slightly.

The surrounding darkness begins revealing water.

### Camera

Slowly moves toward the light.

### Important

The light should remain small.

Do not immediately reveal the whole scene.

Mystery first.

---

# Shot 02.3 · Water

The camera passes through the darkness and discovers water.

The water occupies the foreground.

Moon reflection is not fully visible yet.

### Motion

Water moves continuously.

Reflection reacts subtly to the camera.

### Depth

Foreground:

water reflection

Midground:

mist

Background:

mountains

Far background:

sky

---

# Shot 02.4 · Moon Reveal

The camera rises.

The moon slowly appears above the mountain ridge.

This is the first major visual reveal.

Do NOT:

> moon opacity 0 → 1.

Instead:

> camera movement reveals moon behind mountain.

This is important.

---

# Shot 02.5 · Bridge

The camera continues forward.

The magpie bridge is initially hidden behind mist.

As the camera shifts:

> bridge emerges.

A tiny line of light runs across the bridge.

Not a glow explosion.

It should feel like:

> an old bridge recognizing the visitor.

---

# Shot 02.6 · Butterfly

Only after the world is established does the butterfly enter.

It should enter from outside the screen.

Not appear at center.

Flight:

```text
outside frame
     ↓
foreground
     ↓
passes camera
     ↓
moves toward bridge
```

The camera does not immediately follow.

There is a slight delay.

Then:

> camera begins following.

This creates temporal depth.

---

# Shot 02.7 · Touch the Bridge

The CTA should be visually integrated into the bridge.

The button is not an HTML button floating above the scene.

It should feel like:

> a seal / invitation / magical mark.

When touched:

```text
button press
→ bridge light
→ water ripple
→ tiny particles
→ sound
→ camera begins movement
```

This is the actual beginning of the story.

---

# 03 · 相逢鹊渡

## Emotional purpose

This is not:

> “Here is the female character.”

and:

> “Here is the male character.”

It is:

> **two beings gradually discovering that they exist in the same world.**

The scene should feel like destiny slowly becoming visible.

---

# Shot 03.1 · Establishing World

### Composition

```text
             Moon

        far mountains

             mist

       near mountains

~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
          water

         bridge

foreground branches
```

### Camera

Very wide.

Almost static.

But the world breathes.

### Motion

Moon:

extremely slow drift.

Mountains:

almost static.

Mist:

slow lateral movement.

Water:

continuous.

Foreground:

slightly faster.

This establishes depth before anything important happens.

---

# Shot 03.2 · Butterfly Enters

The butterfly appears from the lower-left / side of the frame.

It should not immediately become the center of attention.

For a moment:

> the viewer discovers it themselves.

### Butterfly motion

Not linear.

Use a natural curved trajectory.

Its body follows the tangent of its path.

Its altitude gently oscillates.

Its wing movement is subtle.

### Camera

Initially remains still.

Then begins a gentle tracking motion.

The delay is intentional.

---

# Shot 03.3 · Crossing the River

The butterfly travels across the water.

This is an important depth shot.

The butterfly should pass through:

```text
foreground mist
→
middle air
→
bridge distance
```

The water below reacts only subtly.

The camera moves laterally.

The moon drifts in the opposite apparent direction.

This makes the world feel three-dimensional.

---

# Shot 03.4 · First Hint of Him

The viewer should not immediately see the magpie clearly.

Instead:

far away:

> tiny dark silhouette.

At first the viewer may not even recognize it.

The butterfly continues moving.

The camera slightly pulls backward.

This creates more space.

Then the silhouette becomes recognizable.

---

# Shot 03.5 · Magpie Reveal

The magpie approaches.

The camera does NOT chase it immediately.

Instead:

> the magpie moves through the scene while the camera watches.

Then camera begins tracking.

This difference is important.

It makes the creature feel like it exists independently of the camera.

---

# Shot 03.6 · Two Paths

The butterfly and magpie should occupy different parts of the composition.

Their trajectories are not identical.

Think:

```text
butterfly
     \
      \
       \

              /

             /
        magpie
```

They are not yet meeting.

They are moving toward the same place.

This is the first visual representation of:

> **双向奔赴**

but in its smallest form.

---

# Shot 03.7 · Bridge Awakening

When both approach the bridge:

the bridge becomes active.

Light begins from one side.

```text
LEFT → CENTER → RIGHT
```

The light travels across the bridge.

The water catches the reflection.

Mist catches the light.

The surrounding environment subtly warms.

The bridge should appear to have memory.

---

# Shot 03.8 · Meeting

The butterfly reaches the bridge.

The magpie lands.

For a moment:

**everything slows down.**

Camera:

slow push-in.

Background:

slows.

Particles:

reduce.

Water:

still continues.

The world has not stopped.

But attention has narrowed.

This is:

> **the moment they notice each other.**

---

# Shot 03.9 · Title

Only after the meeting:

> **相逢鹊渡**

appears.

Not before.

Typography:

blur → focus.

Then:

> **相守情长**

appears with a slight delay.

Then after another breath:

> **故与君鹊渡情长**

appears.

The final phrase should feel like a conclusion, not a subtitle.

---

# 04 · 相逢之后 → 前世

## Emotional purpose

The viewer should gradually realize:

> This meeting is not the beginning.

It has happened before.

The present world begins to remember the past.

---

# Shot 04.1 · Stillness

After the title, hold the scene.

Do not immediately transition.

The bridge remains.

Butterfly and magpie remain.

Music becomes quieter.

This gives emotional weight to the revelation.

---

# Shot 04.2 · Camera Looks Up

The camera slowly tilts upward.

The bridge falls below.

Water disappears.

Mountains occupy more of the frame.

Clouds enter foreground.

The camera continues rising.

---

# Shot 04.3 · Cloud Passage

A cloud passes directly in front of the camera.

For a moment:

the entire screen is mist / cloud.

This is the transition mask.

Do NOT crossfade to the next scene.

The camera physically travels through the cloud.

---

# Shot 04.4 · World Transformation

As the camera emerges:

the scale has changed.

Mountains are enormous.

The sky is deeper.

The moon is stronger.

The atmosphere is mythological.

Color shifts:

```text
modern blue
→
deep indigo
→
red / gold
```

This should feel like entering a memory older than the characters.

---

# 05 · 前世 · 暮云

## Emotional purpose

Introduce her not as a character card.

Introduce her as:

> **a presence inside a myth.**

---

# Shot 05.1 · Phoenix First

Do not show 暮云 first.

Show:

> golden light.

Then:

> phoenix silhouette.

The phoenix moves through clouds.

Only afterward does the camera reveal 暮云.

This gives her mythological importance.

---

# Shot 05.2 · 暮云 Reveal

She stands in the red long dress.

Camera:

wide three-quarter view.

Do not immediately close up on face.

Let the environment establish her.

Red dress:

wind movement.

Hair:

very subtle.

Sleeves:

slow movement.

Phoenix:

behind / above.

---

# Shot 05.3 · Portrait

Only after establishing her world:

camera moves closer.

The background softens.

The face becomes the visual center.

The phoenix remains blurred behind.

This is the first intimate shot of the past.

---

# 06 · 前世 · 长风

## Emotional purpose

Introduce him as:

> wind arriving from somewhere beyond the mountains.

---

# Shot 06.1 · Wind Before Character

Before showing 长风:

the environment changes.

Clouds move.

Grass bends.

Water ripples.

Leaves move.

The viewer should feel:

> someone is approaching.

---

# Shot 06.2 · Blue Figure

The camera reveals 长风 in the blue long robe.

Do not immediately show his face.

Start from:

robe
→ hand
→ shoulder
→ silhouette
→ face

This is a cinematic reveal.

---

# Shot 06.3 · Kunpeng

The Kunpeng should appear much larger than the human figure.

Prefer distant scale.

It should feel like:

> a creature from the sky.

Not a pet.

Camera slowly pulls back to reveal its size.

---

# 07 · 长风恋暮云

## Emotional purpose

This is the past-life promise.

The two worlds finally occupy the same frame.

---

# Shot 07.1

暮云 on one side.

长风 on the other.

Between them:

mountain / mist / distance.

Do not immediately bring them together.

The distance is important.

---

# Shot 07.2

Camera slowly travels between them.

The red and blue color fields begin to overlap.

Phoenix gold crosses the frame.

Wind crosses the frame.

The two visual motifs begin mixing.

---

# Shot 07.3 · Promise

Text:

> 长风恋暮云

appears.

Then:

> 这是我们曾经的许诺。

The phrase should appear almost like handwriting remembered from another life.

---

# Shot 07.4 · Oath

The lines:

> 君为暮云，我为风，
> 生生世世不相离。

appear while camera moves toward the two.

Then:

> 君为长风，我为云，
> 世世生生不相弃。

The camera reaches the point between them.

Music reaches its first emotional peak.

---

# 08 · 前世 → 今世

This transition is extremely important.

Do not simply change scene.

---

# Shot 08.1

Camera pulls backward.

The mythological world becomes smaller.

Mountains recede.

Phoenix becomes distant.

Kunpeng disappears into clouds.

---

# Shot 08.2

A cloud passes.

When it clears:

same composition.

But:

no phoenix.

no Kunpeng.

No ancient costume.

Instead:

modern landscape.

This is the visual statement:

> **轮回之后，我们又来到人间。**

---

# 09 · 今世

## Emotional purpose

The epic world becomes ordinary.

And ordinary becomes precious.

---

# Shot 09.1 · Human Scale

Camera moves close.

No enormous mountains.

No gigantic creatures.

Instead:

window
light
table
tea
hands.

The camera should feel almost handheld.

Not literally shaky.

But intimate.

---

# Shot 09.2 · Daily Life

Use small motions:

curtain moves.

steam rises.

sunlight changes.

cup is touched.

hand enters frame.

These movements should be much smaller than the mythology.

---

# Shot 09.3 · Two Cups

Two cups.

Camera slowly moves between them.

One cup:

slightly closer to camera.

Second cup:

slightly farther.

Then camera shifts so they occupy the same visual plane.

This quietly represents:

> two people becoming one daily life.

---

# Shot 09.4 · Quiet Days

Text:

> Quiet days, quiet cuddles.

Do not animate dramatically.

Letters should appear almost naturally.

Then:

> 陪伴的日子安宁，坚定的拥抱无声。

The camera barely moves.

Let the viewer rest.

---

# 10 · 山高路远

## Emotional purpose

This is the emotional backbone of the whole website.

It is not a separation scene.

It is:

> **a promise of return.**

---

# Shot 10.1 · Sunset

Wide mountain shot.

The sun is low.

The world is warm.

Camera static.

Wind moves.

The scene should feel like:

> a promise is about to be made.

---

# Shot 10.2 · His Departure

The male figure appears.

Do not immediately show his face.

Start with:

foot
→ road
→ robe/clothes
→ silhouette
→ face.

He turns toward the distant mountains.

Camera moves behind him.

This makes the user travel with him.

---

# Shot 10.3 · Promise

Text appears slowly:

> **你别担心。**

Pause.

Then:

> **太阳落山前**

Pause.

Then:

> **我一定回来。**

The final line should not be immediately animated away.

Hold it.

---

# Shot 10.4 · Journey

Camera tracks him.

Mountains pass.

Foreground trees pass quickly.

Middle mountains pass slowly.

Far mountains barely move.

This should be one of the strongest parallax shots on the site.

The user should physically feel distance.

---

# Shot 10.5 · Waiting

Cut is forbidden.

Instead:

camera travels through a mountain / cloud / foreground tree.

When the foreground clears:

the woman is waiting.

This makes it feel like the camera has traveled from him to her.

---

# Shot 10.6 · Her World

The world around her is almost static.

This is intentional.

She does not chase.

She trusts.

Wind moves her clothes.

The sun slowly descends.

She looks toward the mountain.

---

# Shot 10.7 · Her Answer

Text:

> **不必着急。**

Pause.

> **只要你回来，**

Long pause.

Then:

> **太阳永不落山。**

At this exact moment:

the sun should stop descending.

The sunset becomes visually suspended.

---

# 11 · 凤凰金光

This is the supernatural confirmation of the promise.

---

# Shot 11.1

A distant golden light appears between mountains.

At first:

tiny.

The woman notices it.

Camera moves toward her face.

Then camera turns toward the mountains.

---

# Shot 11.2

The phoenix's golden light travels across the landscape.

Do not make the phoenix suddenly fly across the entire screen.

Instead:

light first.

Silhouette second.

Reflection third.

---

# Shot 11.3

The golden light reaches the village.

Everything briefly becomes warm gold.

# 12 · 锁链断裂

Do not make this violent.

It is symbolic.

The restraints have represented:

distance
fear

fate.

One sound:

> **叮。**

# 13 · 双向奔赴

## Emotional purpose

This is where the entire story changes meaning.

She is not waiting anymore.

He is not merely returning.

They are both moving toward each other.

---

# Shot 13.1 · His Side

Camera behind him.

He sees distant golden light.

He starts running.

Camera follows.

---

# Shot 13.2 · Her Side

Camera behind her.

She runs toward the mountains.

Foreground passes quickly.

Hair and dress move.

---

# Shot 13.3 · Alternating Tracking

Do not cut rapidly.

Use long spatial transitions.

Camera:

him
→
mountain
→
her
→
valley
→
him
→
her

The viewer gradually realizes:

their paths are converging.

---

# Shot 13.4 · Wide Reveal

Camera rises.

Now both are visible.

Very far apart.

This is the key shot.

The viewer can see:

> they are running toward each other.

---

# Shot 13.5 · Acceleration

Both figures move faster.

Foreground motion increases.

Music rises.

Camera begins moving toward the meeting point.

---

# Shot 13.6 · Final Approach

Distance becomes smaller.

Camera slows.

Characters slow.

Everything enters a slight slow-motion feeling.

Do not use obvious slow-motion effects.

Instead reduce movement speed.

---

# Shot 13.7 · Embrace

They meet.

The embrace is not a flash.

It is:

approach
→
touch
→
pause
→
embrace.

Then:

camera slowly pulls away.

---

# Shot 13.8 · Eternal Sunset

The sun remains at the horizon.

It does not set.

Water reflects the golden light.

Mountains become quiet.

This is the visual realization of:

> **只要你回来，太阳永不落山。**

---

# 14 · Silence

This is mandatory.

After the embrace:

remove most motion.

Music falls away.

Particles reduce.

Camera almost stops.

Only:

water

This silence is part of the climax.

Do not immediately move to another page.

# 19 · Global Camera Rules

The camera should have a vocabulary.

Do not use one camera motion everywhere.

Use:

### Establish

Wide static shot.

### Discover

Slow pan.

### Approach

Dolly in.

### Reveal

Move behind foreground.

### Follow

Track subject.

### Intimacy

Slow push-in.

### Memory

Vertical rise.

### Distance

Pull back.

### Reunion

Accelerate toward subject, then slow.

### Ending

Long pull-back.

---

# 20 · Global Parallax Rules

For each scene:

### Background

Almost static.

### Midground

Slow movement.

### Subject

Moderate movement.

### Foreground

Fast movement.

### Camera

Independent movement.

### Atmosphere

Continuous drift.

The layers should never move as one flat image.

---

# 21 · Global Temporal-Lag Rules

Every major scene should contain at least three temporal response speeds:

### Immediate

Foreground.

### Medium

Subjects.

### Delayed

Background.

This creates:

> **时差感。**

The viewer should perceive that different objects occupy different temporal depths.

---

# 22 · Global Lighting Rules

Lighting should tell the story.

Opening:

cold moonlight.

Meeting:

blue + silver.

Past:

deep blue + red + gold.

Present:

warm natural light.

Journey:

golden sunset.

Reunion:

warm gold.

Quiet Days:

soft morning light.

Ending:

moon + warm horizon.

---

# 23 · Global Transition Rules

Preferred:

camera travels through objects.

Examples:

```text
mountain
→ cloud
→ present

water
→ reflection
→ memory

sun
→ light
→ phoenix

bridge
→ camera passes through
→ next chapter
```

Avoid:

```text
fade black
fade white
hard cut
section swap
```

unless deliberately used for emotional punctuation.

---

# 24 · Global Typography Rules

Important text should behave like part of cinematography.

Never make all text appear simultaneously.

Use:

```text
line 1
    ↓
pause
    ↓
line 2
    ↓
pause
    ↓
emotional line
```

Important phrases should have more time.

Especially:

> 长风恋暮云。

> 这是我们曾经的许诺。

> 你别担心。

> 太阳落山前我一定回来。

> 不必着急。

> 只要你回来，太阳永不落山。

> 相逢鹊渡。

> 相守情长。

---

# 25 · Global Button Direction

Buttons are not UI widgets.

They are:

> **objects inside the story world.**

Every button should have:

### Rest

Almost invisible.

### Awareness

Subtle glow / movement.

### Hover

Physical response.

### Press

Compression.

### Release

Small rebound.

### Transition

The button's action should cause a world response.

Example:

Bridge button:

```text
press
→ ripple
→ bridge glow
→ camera movement
→ next shot
```

Letter button:

```text
press
→ seal reacts
→ paper opens
```

Ending button:

```text
press
→ camera continues into distance
```

---

# 26 · Mobile Cinematography

Mobile is not a reduced desktop.

It is a different camera.

On mobile:

* characters may become larger
* foreground becomes more important
* camera movement becomes more vertical
* text occupies smaller width
* composition becomes portrait-oriented

Never simply crop the desktop composition.

Recompose the shot.

---

# 27 · Quality Standard

A scene passes only if:

### On slow scrolling

The user can discover details.

### On medium scrolling

The scene feels cinematic.

### On fast scrolling

The scene remains coherent.

### On reverse scrolling

The scene can naturally rewind.

# 28 · The Final Test

Ask:

> If all text disappeared, would the user still understand what is happening?

If no:

the scene depends too much on text.

Ask:

> If Three.js disappeared and everything became flat DOM, would the scene lose something important?

If no:

Three.js is not being used meaningfully.

Ask:

> If all opacity animations were removed, would the scene still work?

If no:

the animation system is too weak.

Ask:

> Can I tell where the camera is?

If no:

the scene lacks cinematography.

Ask:

> Can I feel distance?

If no:

the scene lacks depth.

Ask:

> Can I feel that two people are moving toward each other?

If no:

the story has not been successfully animated.
